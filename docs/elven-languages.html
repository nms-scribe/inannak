Elven Languages

Old Elven (älveen /ælʲβʲe̞ːnʲ/) is a classical language originally spoken in the ancient Elven Dominion, and is used as a literary, scriptural and legal language in many lands. It is the proto-language for numerous modern Elven and Goblin dialects.

It is still spoken in official Elvish political, legal and religious situations, from special ceremonies to day-to-day court and imperial business. In these situations, speakers are expected to follow a pronunciation and vocabulary that is believed to have not changed since the first civilizations emerged. Meetings often include a licensed shun (language teacher, interpreter and corrector, plural shunimopi) on hand to ensure that this is done. This job of correcting the language is taken seriously. There is an Elven saying translated as, "emperors, kings and princes must nod to the shun in matters of speech".

The language is an agglutinative and synthetic language. Combinations of prefixes and suffixes are added to roots to form words. In Old Elven, each change in category such as gender, person, number, or tense is handled by a different affix, whereas in an inflective language, which the reader may be more famliar with, a single affix might be used to indicate a single combination of all of those.

Word order in Old Elven is generally subject-object-verb, and adjectives come after nouns. The language recognizes makes use of four genders: female, male, animate neutral and inanimate neutral.

Plurals. There are four plurals used in Elven, formed by the suffixes listed under Pronouns below, regardless of gender. The simple plural can be used anywhere where the number is known to be more than one, but otherwise unspecified. But if the number is known or estimated with some precision, then another plural may be appropriate. The small plural is used when the number is known to be small. The large plural is used when the number is known to be larger. The total plural is used when the word represents all in existence, or all present. The choice of suffix is context-dependent, as a small number of grains of sand may be much larger than a small number of larger objects.

NOTE

Old Elven is described here for use in building single words such as proper nouns for personal use. It is not intended to be a spoken or literary language, and thus the grammar is not described.
