The World of Inannak

Languages of Inannak

There are hundreds to thousands of different languages on Inannak. No book exists which could describe every single one. Languages can differ greatly by species, class and especially region. Their forms can vary as much as the spoken languages of the Anthropoid species to the pheremones of the Mantids. They change over time, slowly from generation to generation but after enough time has passed two villages twenty miles apart can grow from dialects to unintelligble languages. Included in this book is summary information only on a few of the more influential languages.

Language Capabilities

The author assumes that the reader is human, because of what language this is written in. If so, they may have a certain sense about what the term language means. In this definition, language may mean speaking sounds with their mouth and listening with their ears. But these vocal languages, as they are understood, are limited to certain sounds which can be made with the anthropic mouth. They are also limited by what the anthropic ear can hear, and what their minds can discern as words.

On Inannak, such vocal languages, as the reader understands them, are usable only by Elves, Humans and Orghu. These species have the same shape of mouths and ears, and think similarly enough that they can learn to understand and speak each others languages. There may still be some slight differences between languages of each species, including concepts and sounds that will be forever be incomprehensible to the others. But these are few and far between.

However, this is not the case with the other species, whose different shaped faces, mouths, ears and throats make them unable to use the same sorts of sounds to communicate.

Gesture Languages. Many individuals who for various reasons are unable to vocalize or hear the same as others in their species, use languages that depend on gestures, postures and facial expressions. These are distinct from the vocal languages, having different grammars and words. The same anatomical differences that effect vocal languages effect these communication methods as well. For example, at least among anthropic species, facial expressions are an important part of these types of languages. However, Gnoles and Shirrwern faces are incapable of moving in the same ways, making it difficult to include them. On the other hand, they have body movements that anthropic species are unable to mimic as well, such as the use of a tail, or the ability to make use of four instead of just two arms.

Gnoles

Gnole vocalizations are less dependent on the ability to shape sounds in the mouth. Instead, they use variance in pitch, volume and phoneme length to match the variety of sounds of anthropic languages. They also add to this inventory sharp barks, quiet whines of various pitches and lengths, and many qualities of growls and snuffles. Gestures and posture are much more important parts of grammar and tone than they are among anthropic species. Even written languages must include symbols representing these parts, or meanings of those written words are lost to the ages.

Shirrwern

Shirrwern have the capability of speaking and hearing pitches that anthropic ears, and some Gnole ears, can not hear. While they are as limited in vocal-shaping as the Gnoles, they make use of varieties of clicking, chittering and chirping sounds that the anthropic mouth can not make. One feature extensive in their language is the use of rhythm and speed of utterances to differentiate meaning.

Saurians

To anthropic ears, the Saurian vocalizations range from that of chirping and squawking of birds to croaking of frogs. However, whereas those creatures usually only each know a few variations of sounds they can make, Saurian languages are full of thousands of such songs. Saurian vocalizations do not break down into simple sounds, as anthropic languages do. Instead, each word is made of many variations and combinations of sound that can not be separated from each other. The result is hard to explain, but perhaps an understanding can be had by readers who know the difference between a logographic writing system and an alphabet.

Sea Peoples

The vocal languages of the Sea Peoples are the most alien of sounds to anthropic ears. While some of the Sea People have the appearance of anthropic anatomy, their throats are shaped different due to their ability to switch between water and air environments. To anthropic ears, Sea People vocalizations sound like a kind of etheric singing, full of sounds shifting slowly pitch and volumne, with occasional chirps and laughter. The sound is different in the acoustics of the ocean compared to above the surface, but fluent listeners are still able to understand in either environment. Like the Gnoles, the Sea Peoples include body movements in their languages, but in a different way. Underwater, Sea People are in constant motion, and their language includes in it their speed and direction of swim, their position above or below the listener, and even the force of touch between the speaker and the listener.

Mantids

Among the Liberated Species, the Mantids do not and can not use vocal languages. They are capable of making sounds with their bodies, usually made by clacking of mandibles, or rubbing limbs against their exoskeletons. But these sounds are not used for speaking any more than a hand clap is language for a human. Many are also unable to understand the vocalizations of other species. They can't even learn to understand.

Instead, the Mantids use something which is translated as Essence in Freetongue. This is sometimes described as a complex layering of scents. But the essence seems to travel faster through the air, and the Mantids are capable of directing it to listeners or broadcasting it over a wide audience. It is effected by strong winds, however. This silent communication often leads some individuals of other species to believe the Mantids can speak mind-to-mind as the gods could. But this is not true.

Just as no other species can understand their Essence, they seem to be unable to discern the differences in vocalizations enough to understand the language of other species. Thus, when in mixed company, they must use gesture languages.

In order to facilitate communication with other species, mixed societies with Mantids often develop a gesture language that makes use of the limited anatomical similarities of the speakers. These languages, called Mantid Trade Hands, are entirely based on postures and arm and hand movement only of the upper two arms of the Mantids. Mantids can not make facial expressions and flexible finger movements a non-Mantid could easily interpret, and non-Mantids are usually limited to two hands. Features which depend on these specialties are not a part of these languages.

Major Languages

Freetongue

This book is written in a language called Freetongue. This is a creole that is spoken in many of the ports and nations that border the Mesos Ocean. It developed first from human languages along the northern coasts, and spread with trade and military conquest across the ocean. Although it is a second language to most, it has developed into a first language in many cosmopolitan locations. It is a literary language as well, having been adopted by several large universities due to its ubiquity.

Freehand

The Freetongue language is of anthropic origin, and there are many who can not speak it. In order for those species and individuals to participate in commerce, a gesture language parallel to Freetongue has developed. The language is similar to several well-known Mantid Trade Hands in form, which is probably where it developed. As with all such languages, finger usage is limited, making the gestures of Freehand relying on more elaborate posturing and arm and wrist movement than anthropic gesture languages.

Elven Languages

The Elven languages make up a large proportion of languages spoken by the Elven Empire and related cultures. The stable core of the family is known as Old Elven, which is about 10,000 years old. A closely similar language is Forest Elven, which most believe to be a corrupted dialect of the former. Yet it is the language of day to day business among Elven society, especially the many not trained in Old Elven. Unlike Old Elven, but like most languages, it is full of regional dialects and slang.

The languages of the shorter-lived Elven castes are much more numerous, and change quickly compared to the two traditional Elven languages. These languages differ over time and by region almost as much as Human languages. Many lower castes, especially among the Firbolgs and Goblins, speak numerous regional languages which do not seem to be similar at all to Elven, except for a few probably coincidental similarly sounding words.

The languages of the Deep Elves parallel that of their above-ground fellows. While the lower castes speak numerous tongus, a language called Deep Elven is spoken among the higher castes which has taken a different path in history. The sounds of Deep Elven are entirely different, as the lexicon seems to be. However, scholars who study both Deep Elven and Old Elven have noticed unusual similarities, such as words for the same thing where the phonemes or syllables have been reversed. It is believed that when the Deep Elves moved into the Underneath, they purposely changed their language, speaking words backwards, in order to differentiate themselves from their ancestors.

Magical Languages

When the gods created spells for mortals to use, they often required them to be triggered by linguistic signals. In order to avoid accidentally triggering a spell in ordinary language, these sounds were designed to be specifically different from regular language. For the same reason, they were often combined with gestures that would not often be made at the same time as those words.

When Eilwain created the spells for her books, she also created a language that is known by many as the Song of Eilwain, or the Language of the Hebdomad. This has become the language which most spells are spoken in today. It is also the source of several more arcane words, such as the name of the world, Inannak. After it's creation it was adopted by the rest of the hebdomad as a religious language which priests and other mortals were required to use when communicating directly to the gods in prayer or in person.

This language is not the only such magical language. There are some ancient spells which may pre-date this creation which require completely different sounds or gestures. Some of these show their origins by including gestures and vocalizations from non-anthropic species.
